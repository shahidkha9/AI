## 0. How sources were checked and what "verified" means

This session cannot open Google Scholar, Crossref/DOI services or publisher websites directly, so every source was checked through web searches that returned **publisher pages, library catalogues (e.g. WorldCat, British Library), journal websites or repository records**.

| Mark | Meaning | May it be cited? |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ | Authors, year, title and venue confirmed by at least one publisher, journal, catalogue or repository record | Yes |
| 🟡 | The source exists, but **one detail** is uncertain (named in the table) | Yes, **after** that detail is checked |
| ❌ | Could not be confirmed, or details conflict | **No.** Removed |

**Important limit:** "verified" means the **source exists with these details**. In most cases **only the abstract** has been seen. Before Chapter 2 reports any study's *findings*, the full text (or at least the abstract) must be read, and findings will be reported **only as far as that reading supports**. Any claim drawn only from an abstract will be worded accordingly (e.g. "the abstract reports…").

## 6. What the verified literature shows (preliminary map for Chapter 2)

This is based on titles and abstracts only and will be checked when the full texts are read.

1. **CDA of Pakistani print newspapers is well established.** Several studies analyse political headlines in *Dawn*, *The Nation* and *Express* using van Dijk's or Fairclough's models (P1, P2, P3).
2. **The common comparison is English vs Urdu, or one newspaper vs another**, not **digital-native vs print** (P2, P3).
3. **Pakistani CDA of digital news is scarce and small-scale.** The one verified study (P4) analyses four Facebook headlines.
4. **International research on online vs print news is mostly quantitative content analysis**, not CDA, and its findings conflict. Ghersetti (J4) found no significant difference, while Deuze (J1) and Karlsson (J2) argue that online production changes journalism.

**Provisional research gap (to be confirmed after full reading):** In the searches carried out, **no study was found that uses CDA to compare Pakistani digital-native outlets with *Dawn*'s print edition on the same political event.** The thesis will word this carefully ("no study was identified in the literature reviewed") rather than claiming that no such study exists anywhere.
