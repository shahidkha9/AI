## 1. The scoping problem and how this proposal solves it

The title sets "digital media" against *Dawn*, but *Dawn* also publishes on Dawn.com and on social media. If the term "digital media" is left undefined, the two sides of the comparison overlap, and any difference found could not be explained by the medium. The study therefore needs a definition of "digital media" that does **not** include *Dawn*, and a definition of "*Dawn*" that refers to one clearly identified form of its output.

Three ways of doing this are possible:

| Option | "Digital media" means | "*Dawn*" means | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A (recommended)** | English-language **digital-native** Pakistani news outlets, i.e. outlets that publish only online and have no print newspaper | Reports from *Dawn*'s **print edition** (identified by the line "Published in Dawn, [date]" on the Dawn.com version, or by the e-paper) | Clean contrast between an online-only newsroom and a legacy print newspaper; same genre (news reports) and same language on both sides; texts are public and easy to cite | Digital-native outlets are smaller, and their archives must be checked before sampling |
| B | **Social-media news posts** (e.g. X or Facebook posts) published by verified news organisations | *Dawn* print reports | Captures the "social media" sense of digital media | Posts are a few lines long and articles are hundreds of words long, so the units are hard to compare fairly; posts can be deleted or edited; much harder to analyse at BS level |
| C | *Dawn*'s own **digital** output (Dawn.com originals, Dawn's social-media posts) | *Dawn*'s print edition | Same organisation, so the comparison isolates the medium | Changes the meaning of the title ("Dawn vs Dawn"). The supervisor would have to approve a changed topic |

**Recommendation: Option A.** It keeps the central meaning of your approved title, makes the comparison defensible in a viva, and gives you texts of similar length and genre.

**Operational definition (Option A):** *In this study, "digital media" refers to English-language Pakistani news outlets that publish exclusively online and do not produce a print newspaper. "*Dawn*" refers to news reports that appeared in the print edition of the daily* Dawn.

## 4. Draft statement of the problem

> Pakistani readers now receive political news from two very different kinds of newsroom: long-established newspapers such as *Dawn*, with an editorial tradition shaped by print, and newer outlets that exist only online. Research on Pakistani news discourse has largely examined print newspapers, often comparing English and Urdu dailies, while the language of digital-native outlets has received far less systematic attention. As a result, it is not clear whether these outlets construct political actors and events differently from a legacy newspaper, or whether they reproduce the same patterns of attribution, evaluation and framing. This study addresses that gap by comparing how selected digital-native outlets and *Dawn* represented [the selected event], using Critical Discourse Analysis to examine both the linguistic choices in the texts and the institutional and social conditions in which they were produced.

*The claim that digital-native outlets have received less attention is a working assumption. It must be confirmed by the literature search in Stage 4. If the search shows otherwise, this paragraph will be rewritten.*

## 6. Theoretical framework

**Main framework: Norman Fairclough's three-dimensional model of Critical Discourse Analysis.** It is one framework with three levels, and each level matches part of the research questions:

| Fairclough's dimension | What it examines | Research question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **Text** (description) | Vocabulary, grammar (e.g. active/passive), headlines, quotation | RQ1, RQ2 |
| **Discursive practice** (interpretation) | How the text is produced and consumed: sources quoted, intertextuality, online vs print production routines | RQ2, RQ3 |
| **Social practice** (explanation) | How the discourse relates to power, ideology and the political context | RQ4 |

**Supporting concept: Teun A. van Dijk's "ideological square".** This is the pattern of emphasising positive things about "us" and negative things about "them", and playing down the reverse. It is used **only** at the third level, as a tool for checking whether ideological polarisation is present. It is not a second framework.

**Analytical tools taken from these frameworks:**
- Nomination (how actors are named) and predication (what qualities are attributed to them)
- Transitivity and agency (who does what to whom; active vs passive; nominalisation)
- Lexical evaluation (positive or negative word choice)
- Headline structure and emphasis
- Attribution and source selection (who is quoted, how reporting verbs are chosen: *said / claimed / admitted*)
- Presupposition, metaphor and intertextuality

**Why not more theories?** Using Fairclough, van Dijk, Wodak and framing theory as four full frameworks would look impressive but would be shallow at BS level. One main model and one supporting concept is easier to apply consistently and easier to defend in a viva.

## 7. Preliminary methodology

| Element | Proposal |
|---|---|
| Research approach | Qualitative |
| Research design | Comparative Critical Discourse Analysis |
| Population | All English-language news reports on [the event] published by *Dawn* (print) and Pakistani digital-native outlets during the time window |
| Sample | 16 news reports (8 + 8), selected purposively against the criteria in Section 3 |
| Instrument | An **analytical framework / coding sheet** based on Fairclough's three dimensions. The supervisor's slides list "analytical framework" as an accepted instrument (D2, slide 21) |
| Data collection | Texts copied from the official websites; URL, date, author and access date recorded; each page also saved in the Internet Archive (Wayback Machine) so the examiner can verify it later |
| Data analysis | Each text coded line by line → findings tabulated per outlet → paired comparison → interpretation at the discursive and social levels |
| Ethics | No human participants, so consent is not needed. Texts are public. Quotations will be exact and in context. The analysis will not make claims about journalists' personal intentions. The researcher's own political views will be kept out of the analysis |
| Trustworthiness | **Credibility**: every claim is linked to a quotation. **Dependability**: the coding sheet is applied to all 16 texts in the same way. **Confirmability**: the full corpus is listed in an appendix. **Transferability**: the context is described so readers can judge whether the findings apply elsewhere |
| Limitations | Small sample; one event; English-language outlets only; the findings cannot be generalised to all Pakistani media |
