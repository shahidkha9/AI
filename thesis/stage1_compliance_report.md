## 4. Content requirements that will shape the research (D2, D4, D5)

- **Objectives:** action-oriented, specific, achievable, linked to the problem statement; verbs such as *analyse, explore, examine, investigate*. Each objective must correspond to one research question (D2 slides 8–9).
- **Research questions:** linked to objectives, clear, focused, researchable, answerable through data analysis (D2 slide 9); open-ended and arguable rather than factual (D4 slide 6).
- **Problem statement:** specific, clear, researchable; names the issue, the gap, limits of earlier studies and the need for this study (D2 slide 6).
- **Scope and delimitations:** must state what is covered and what is excluded — selected texts, specific time period, specific theory (D2 slide 10).
- **Literature review:** analytical, not descriptive; journal articles, books, theses, conference papers; end with a gap — theoretical, contextual, methodological or population gap (D2 slides 12–14).
- **Linguistics studies:** data source may be social media data or corpora; design is discourse analysis or corpus analysis; the "instrument" may be an analytical framework (D2 slides 17–23). This confirms that a CDA study of news texts fits the department's model.
- **Sampling:** name the type (purposive, convenience, random) and explain sample size and selection criteria (D2 slide 20).
- **Qualitative quality criteria:** credibility, dependability, confirmability, transferability (D2 slide 24).
- **Topic narrowing:** a topic must be focused, researchable and manageable within the word count; avoid vague topics; write a 1–2 page proposal and consult the supervisor early (D5 slides 6, 9, 10).
- **Final submission checklist (D2 slide 30):** five chapters complete · APA 7 followed · research gap identified · objectives and questions aligned · methodology explained · findings discussed · references complete · declaration of originality included.

## 7. Early warning for Stage 2 (no decisions made yet)

The topic as worded is broader than D5 recommends, and it contains one conceptual problem that must be solved before the research design is fixed:

1. **Dawn is itself digital.** Dawn publishes on Dawn.com and on social media. "Digital media vs Dawn" therefore needs a precise definition — for example, *digital-native news outlets* versus *Dawn's print/e-paper edition*. Otherwise an examiner can say the comparison overlaps.
2. **No event or period is set.** A CDA thesis of about 12,000 words usually works best with **one political event or issue** in a **short, fixed time window**.
3. **Sample size.** For a qualitative CDA at BS level, a corpus of roughly 10–20 texts (split evenly between the two sides) is normally manageable. This is a planning estimate, not a departmental rule.
4. **Ethics.** If social media posts by private individuals are included, anonymity becomes an ethical issue (D2 slide 24). Using only posts from verified news organisations avoids this.

I will propose options for each of these in Stage 2, and I will not select any source, event or date without your approval.
